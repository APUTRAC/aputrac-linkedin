## Post

Fuel is about a quarter of what it costs to run an airline.

IATA's June 2025 outlook put the industry's fuel bill at $236 billion, 25.8% of operating costs. [SOURCE: iata.org pressroom, 2 Jun 2025. Check for a newer outlook and use the latest.]

You can't control the price of fuel. You can control how much you burn while the aircraft isn't going anywhere.

Studies put the APU at roughly 1 to 2.5% of an airline's fuel burn [SOURCE: IPCC aviation report and academic paper; confirm]. Even at the low end, 1% of $236 billion is more than $2 billion a year across the industry. [This is simple arithmetic on the two figures above: label it as such.]

The part almost nobody manages is not the APU itself. It's the minutes it runs beyond what the operation actually needs: waiting on a remote stand, left running overnight, running at a gate that has ground power and air.

[OPTIONAL: Our estimate is that unmanaged APU use wastes $700M+ a year industry-wide. SOURCE or METHOD needed. If this is APUTRAC's own model, say "we estimate" and be ready to explain how.]

Here's a question for the fuel and ops people reading: what share of your fuel bill do you think goes to APU use? Take a guess in the comments, then tell me next week whether you can actually see the real number.
