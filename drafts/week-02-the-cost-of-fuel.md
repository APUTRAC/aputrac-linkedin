## Post

Fuel is now almost a third of what it costs to run an airline.

IATA's June 2026 outlook puts the industry's fuel bill at about $350 billion this year, 31.4% of operating costs, up from about 25% in 2025. Jet fuel is averaging $152 a barrel. [SOURCE: IATA, June 2026]

Airlines can't control the price. They can control how much they burn while the aircraft isn't going anywhere.

IATA expects airlines to burn about 104 billion gallons in 2026. Studies put the APU at roughly 1 to 2.5% of an airline's fuel. My own best estimate is about 1.5%.

That is roughly 1.5 billion gallons, and more than $5 billion, burned by auxiliary power units, mostly while aircraft sit on the ground.

Much of that is needed. The waste is the minutes beyond what the operation actually needs: waiting on a remote stand, running overnight, running at a gate that has ground power and air. If roughly one gallon in eight of that is avoidable, that's about $700 million a year. [OPTIONAL: only if the $700M+ claim is confirmed]

Question for the fuel and ops people reading: what share of your fuel bill do you think goes to APU use? Take a guess in the comments, and I'll share how I got to my number.

## First comment
How I got there: 104 billion gallons x about 1.5% = about 1.56 billion gallons, at about $3.37 a gallon (IATA's $350B / 104B gal) = about $5.3 billion. The 1.5% is my estimate; studies range from 1% to 2.5%.
Source: IATA, June 2026 outlook: https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-07-middle-east-disruptions-high-fuel-prices-halve-airline-industry-profitability/

## Notes and facts to verify
- All IATA numbers come from news coverage of the June 2026 outlook. Check them against IATA's press release and presentation (links in `apu-fuel-estimate.md`), and look for any later update.
- The APU share (1.5%) is my estimate, not an IATA figure. The 1 to 2.5% range comes from an IPCC report (about 1% for British Airways) and an academic paper (2 to 2.5%, which cites IATA but I could not confirm it). Say "my estimate" in the post.
- "$3.37 a gallon" is IATA's fuel bill divided by its consumption: an average after hedging, not a market price.
- "One gallon in eight" is about 13%. It follows from $700M / $5.3B. Keep it only if the $700M+ claim is confirmed.
