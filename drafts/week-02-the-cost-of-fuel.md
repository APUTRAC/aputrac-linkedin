## Post

Fuel is now almost a third of what it costs to run an airline.

Any airline deciding whether to act on APU fuel should start from its own APU fuel cost and the savings it can realistically expect. Before we get to yours, here are the industry numbers, to show the scale.

IATA's June 2026 outlook puts the industry's fuel bill at about $350 billion this year, 31.4% of operating costs.

Airlines can't control the price. Some soften it by hedging, but IATA says only about a third of expected 2026 fuel is hedged worldwide. What every airline can control is how much it burns while the aircraft isn't going anywhere.

Published estimates put the APU at roughly 1 to 2.5% of an airline's fuel. My own best estimate is about 1.5%. Applied to IATA's forecast of 104 billion gallons, that is roughly 1.5 billion gallons, and about $5 billion, burned by auxiliary power units, mostly while aircraft sit on the ground.

Much of that is needed. The waste is the minutes beyond what the operation actually needs: waiting on a remote stand, running overnight, running at a gate that has ground power and air.

Question for the fuel and ops people reading: what share of your fuel bill do you think goes to APU use? Take a guess in the comments, and I'll share how I got to my number.

## First comment
How I got there: 104 billion gallons x about 1.5% = about 1.56 billion gallons, at about $3.37 a gallon (IATA's $350B / 104B gal) = about $5.3 billion. The 1.5% is my estimate; published figures range from about 1% to 2.5%.
Source: IATA, June 2026 outlook: https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/06-07-middle-east-disruptions-high-fuel-prices-halve-airline-industry-profitability/
