# Week 1: Who I am and the blind spot

Status: Ready for your final review
Planned: Tuesday 3 Nov 2026

## Post

For ten years I led fuel efficiency work at IATA. The biggest gap I found wasn't in the cockpit, the flight plan, or the engines you'd expect.

It was the one engine nobody watches.

Every engine on an aircraft is managed in real time except one: the APU.

When I asked airlines how much APU fuel they burned, I was surprised how few could tell me with any accuracy. And they were surprised to learn how much fuel they were wasting on the ground, and that it was avoidable.

That's the problem in a nutshell. An APU can run at the gate, on a remote stand or overnight in maintenance while no one who could switch it off knows. Most airlines review the data after landing. By then the fuel is gone, and the reason is hard to find.

After 40+ years in airlines, from the chief pilot's office to IATA, and working with more than 100 airlines, I'm convinced APU management is the biggest fuel-saving opportunity most airlines still aren't managing.

Over the next eight weeks I'll share what I've learned: what an APU really burns, where the waste hides, why installing ground power isn't enough, and how to tell where your own operation stands.

If you work in fuel efficiency, ops control or ground ops: what's the one thing you wish you could see about APU use on your ramp?
