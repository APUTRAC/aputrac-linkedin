# Week 1: Who I am and the blind spot

Status: Posted (Pat posted it himself, Tuesday 27 Oct 2026, evening)
Planned: Tuesday 27 Oct 2026 (moved up from 3 Nov). Results so far are in TRACKER.md.

## Post

I spent ten years on fuel efficiency projects for IATA, often leading them. The biggest gap I found wasn't in flying, flight planning, maintenance or ground operations.

It was the one engine nobody watches.

Almost every engine on an aircraft is managed in real time. The exception is the APU.

When I asked airlines how much APU fuel they burned, I was surprised how few could tell me with any accuracy. And they were surprised to learn how much fuel they were wasting on the ground, and that it was avoidable.

That's the problem in a nutshell. An APU can run at the gate, on a remote stand or overnight in maintenance while no one who could switch it off knows. Most airlines review the data after landing. By then the fuel is gone, and the reason is hard to find.

After 40+ years in airlines, from the chief pilot's office at Delta Air Lines to consulting for IATA, and working with more than 100 airlines, I'm convinced APU management is the biggest fuel-saving opportunity most airlines still aren't managing.

I'll be sharing what I've learned about APU fuel use here over the coming weeks.

If you work in fuel efficiency, ops control or ground ops: what's the one thing you wish you could see about APU use on your ramp?
